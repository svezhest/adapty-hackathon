# What a model makes of 100 believable support tickets

A small study that came out of building the demo data. Generated by `scripts/dataset_study.py`; every number below is computed from the records in `data/dataset/` and `data/demo.sqlite`.

**In short.** 200 support tickets were written by `claude-sonnet-5-5` from invented people and real complaint labels. 100 of them went through the real triage agent on `deepseek-v4.1-flash-1017`. In a sample read by `claude-opus-5-5`, the agent's root cause matched the hidden truth of the case in 19 of 22 tickets and stopped short in 3. It answered 2 of 100 tickets in the wrong language and offered free days in 80 of 100, whether or not they would help. Its own checks refused a submission 62 times, and 5 of 97 tickets failed on the first pass. No person judged any of this, and the data is synthetic throughout.

### About the model's name

The name given by the project's owner is `deepseek-v4.1-flash-1017`. The API rejects that name with an error 400, so the run asked for `deepseek-flash`, and every response came back as `deepseek-flash`, with system fingerprint `bc19ba97cbe016479903222bb1578db6`. Both names are in the record. The API does not confirm that they are the same model.

## The tickets

|  | English | Russian | Serbian | All |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Written | 160 | 20 | 20 | 200 |
| In the demo | 80 | 10 | 10 | 100 |

146 are chats with the in-app assistant that ended in a handoff, 54 are emails. A ticket's author writes between 15 and 176 words, with a median of 73.

**Are they all different?** All 200 `voice` notes differ. 14 three-word openings occur more than once, none more than 3 times. 42 of 19,900 pairs share two or more five-word phrases, the worst pair 6, mostly plain phrases such as "app for a cancel button". This measures repeated wording, not whether the tickets feel like different people.

**Are they copied from reviews?** The writers saw labels, never text. Checked against 21,825 reviews: 26 tickets share a run of 6 or more words with some review, the longest is 7 words, none reach 8. Only these counts leave the check, and no review text is stored here.

Handoff reasons written by the authors: refund 71, other 41, asks_for_human 19, profile_mismatch 10, chargeback 5. No chat ended in `threat`: the one card with a threat label became an email. The brief did not offer `repeat_after_grant`. So the data does not exercise either reason.

## What the triage agent did

211 model calls for 100 tickets: 1,506,991 prompt tokens, 253,899 completion tokens, 88% of the prompt served from cache, median call 7.7 s. The DeepSeek balance fell from 50.47 to 49.19 CNY for the whole job. 14 of these calls were replaced by a second run.

**The first pass failed on 5 of 97 tickets** after one correction each; the other 3 ran earlier as a smoke test. All 5 passed on a second try. The agent's own checks refused a submission 62 times:

| Refused because | Times |
| --- | --- |
| a gendered pronoun for the user | 27 |
| `messages_en` has the wrong number of entries | 16 |
| the arguments are not valid JSON | 12 |
| two options with the same action | 4 |
| a reply over the length limit | 2 |
| other schema errors | 1 |

**Its first choice** is what a person sees on top. Refunds are handed to a person most often, which fits a dataset where refunds are the largest handoff reason:

| Action | As first option | Among all options |
| --- | --- | --- |
| store_instructions | 23 | 81 |
| grant_access | 3 | 80 |
| reply_only | 24 | 56 |
| refund_to_human | 41 | 56 |
| ask_user | 9 | 27 |

**Free days as filler.** `grant_access` is among the options in 80 of 100 tickets, also where it cannot help: f-0012 offers 7 days that start after October 2027. It rates 26 of these as high risk, 28 as medium and 26 as low.

**Language.** 2 tickets by authors writing English got replies in another language: f-0030 (Spanish), f-0068 (German). The other 98 were right, including all 10 in Russian and 10 in Serbian. f-0030 quotes a Spanish bank alert, and f-0068 has a German subject and a "Danke schön" in an English message. These words may be the cause. That is untested.

**Confidence** runs from 0.35 to 0.95, median 0.82. It is not calibrated here: there are no labels to calibrate against.

### Reading a sample

22 tickets, random.seed(7), random.sample over the tickets table in stored order. For each: the card's truth against the triage's root cause. hit = the cause matches the truth; partial = the facts are right, the conclusion stops short of the truth.

Result: 19 hit, 3 partial, 0 wrong.

The partial ones: f-0010: Renewal in flight: says no renewal is recorded and that it may have failed, instead of saying access should come back.; f-0069: The same case: calls the first-line assistant's correct reassurance into question.; f-0071: A discounted first year: names the regular price, and leaves the discount as likely because the account shows no offer.

Noticed on the way: f-0028: Option 0 opens with 'Tobias here, a real person from the FitBuddy team'. Tobias is the user's own name, and the sender is unknown.

**How much to trust this.** The reader is a model, `claude-opus-5-5`, and it read with the truth in hand. The chats were written by a model that knew the truth, and the first-line assistant in them states facts about the account, so part of each answer is already in the input. The result shows that the agent can use what it is given. It does not show how it does on real tickets.

## What the analyst found

One real run over the 100 triaged tickets. Finding, in its own words:

> Charged-after-trial on Onboarding - Weekly first is the biggest cluster a paywall can move: 12 users (31 with refunds and cancel questions in the same flow).

Its proposal: Switch the control's weekly-with-3-day-trial offer to the existing annual-with-7-day-trial flow: annual price and its trial end shown up front, no weekly plan as the default.

The counts come from code, and the choice of cluster and the proposal come from the model. The flows were assigned on the cards, so this shows the agent working and says nothing about any real app.

## The numbers on the Analyze screen

9 tickets answered, 1 closed without a reply, 90 waiting. They are what the screen counts from the real triage. The rest of its numbers come from the synthetic chats, and **their shape was chosen to look good in a demo**:

- 800 chats, all solved, with 800 work records: 454 profile reads, 234 store instructions, 112 grant attempts (97 allowed, 407 free days).
- Languages: en 684, sr 59, ru 57. Largest flows: restore_ok 93, trial_end_date 89, renewal_date 84.
- Daily volume grows by 15% a day, and the chats run on for about 14 hours after the newest ticket.
- That is why the week shows machines at 92.3% against 92.2% the week before. The gain depends on the hour the demo starts: 0.2 to 2.0 points over a day. **The rise was tuned**, not measured. With a gentler curve the same tickets showed a fall.

Read the screen as a demonstration of what it can show, not as a result.

## What this does not show, and what was not tried

- No person judged whether the tickets sound real. No native speaker read the Russian or the Serbian: a model did, and the writing agents said themselves that their checks were partial.
- Only 22 of 100 triages were read, against the root cause only. The wording of the replies and the store steps inside them were not checked against the stores' current help pages.
- The chats' first-line assistant was written by `claude-sonnet-5-5`. The real first-line agent, `resolve/frontline.py`, did not produce these chats.
- The other 100 tickets were written and checked, and never triaged. They are only in `data/dataset/tickets.json`.
- Each ticket was triaged once, apart from the second tries, so there is no measure of how much answers vary between runs. Only one model was tried.
- 94 of the tickets were triaged while their accounts held a `country` field that real profiles do not have, removed afterwards. It may have nudged the language. The 6 tickets with the wrong language were run again without it: 3 became correct and 2 did not. The sixth, c-005, was the writer's error: a Spanish ticket on an English card, rewritten in English and run again. The effect on the other tickets is not known.
- No bug reports: the build has no Jira teams configured, so the agent had nowhere to send one and the code drops them. The screen's Jira flow is not shown by this data.
- The accounts are synthetic and their dates are fixed around the tickets, so nothing here tests lookups that fail for real reasons.
- The answers of the 10 closed tickets were written by a model standing in for a person.

## To repeat it

`scripts/build_demo.py` runs the stages `base`, `bots`, `triage`, `decide`, `insights`, `publish`. Only `triage` and `insights` call a model. `scripts/dataset_check.py` holds the checks on the tickets. Card generation needs the label files, which are not in this repo. See `data/dataset/README.md`.
